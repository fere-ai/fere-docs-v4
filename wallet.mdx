Your money starts in your **Fere Wallet**. Each agent you start then trades from its **own wallet**, funded from there. Under the hood, each one is powered by Coinbase's most advanced wallet infrastructure.

## Your Fere Wallet

The Fere Wallet is the cash you hold before it is assigned to an agent. Deposit there, then use **Add money** on an agent to move cash into that agent's wallet. Taking money out of a stopped or archived agent sends it back to the Fere Wallet.

## Agent Wallets (Fere App)

In the Fere app, your money starts in your **Fere Wallet**, and you fund agents from there. Each agent you start then trades from its own wallet: one wallet per agent. The agent can only trade with its wallet — only you can withdraw.

- **Cash** — money waiting to be traded, shown in US dollars.
- **Holdings** — whatever the agent currently holds from its trades.
- **Activity** — every trade, deposit, and withdrawal for that wallet.

You can see each agent's wallet on its page, and all of them together under **Portfolio**: in the top navigation on a computer, or the first tab on a phone. See [On Your Phone](/phone) for the phone layout, including **Hide balances**.
### If holdings say temporarily unavailable

Balances come from live on-chain providers. When those providers are slow or overloaded, Portfolio and agent wallets show **Holdings temporarily unavailable** instead of a $0 balance.

- Your funds are not moved or lost.
- Use **Retry** on the holdings panel, or refresh holdings from Portfolio, and try again in a minute.

### Archived strategies

A strategy you have closed can still hold funds. Those wallets appear under **Archived strategies** until you take the money out, which sends it back to your Fere Wallet. Empty ones disappear on their own.

### If holdings say temporarily unavailable

Balances come from live on-chain providers. When those providers are slow or overloaded, Portfolio and agent wallets show **Holdings temporarily unavailable** (or **Temporarily unavailable** on the compact balance row) instead of inventing a $0 balance.

- Your funds are not moved or lost.
- The app does **not** keep hammering a failing holdings request in the background.
- Use **Retry** on the holdings panel, refresh holdings from Portfolio, or leave the page and come back when the network is quieter.

### Add funds

Money comes into Fere through your Fere Wallet. On the add-money screen you can:

- **Send crypto** to your Fere Wallet address. Pick the **EVM wallet** (Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum, BNB Chain) or the **Solana wallet**, and send on the network you picked. A QR code is shown for each address.
- **Send from a connected wallet**, approving the transfer in that wallet.
- **Pay with card or bank** through a partner that sells you crypto delivered to your Fere Wallet.

Deposits land as **Cash** (USDC on Base). Then choose **Add money** on an agent's page to move Cash into its wallet. The screen shows the agent's minimum.

**Topping up an agent that trades on Hyperliquid.** An agent that trades on Hyperliquid can only use money that is on Hyperliquid. Money you add lands as Cash in the agent's wallet first, and Fere moves it to Hyperliquid only once that Cash reaches the agent's **minimum** (the amount on the funding screen):

- **At or above the minimum**: Fere moves all of the Cash to Hyperliquid, and the agent grows its open positions in proportion. For example, an agent with $50 on Hyperliquid that holds a $48 short and gets $60 grows the short to about $105.
- **Below the minimum**: the money stays as Cash in the agent's wallet. The agent does not grow its positions with it, because that Cash can't back a trade until it is on Hyperliquid.
- **Small top-ups add up.** Once the Cash waiting in the wallet reaches the minimum, the next deposit or the agent's next trade moves all of it to Hyperliquid. For example, a $20 top-up waits as Cash; add $40 more and the full $60 is moved.

To put a small amount to work sooner, add enough to bring the Cash up to the minimum. The deposit email for a top-up that waits tells you how much Cash is waiting, the minimum that moves it, and how much more to add.

### Withdraw

Choose **Withdraw** on the agent's page, enter an amount in USD, and the address to send it to. If the agent is holding positions rather than Cash, sell them back to Cash first. You can withdraw whether the agent is running or stopped.

**Agents that trade on Hyperliquid.** When an agent's money is on Hyperliquid, Withdraw sends it straight from Hyperliquid to the address you paste, as **USDC on Arbitrum** — in one step, with nothing passing back through the agent's wallet. Use an EVM address you control that can receive on Arbitrum (not an exchange deposit address for another network). Hyperliquid takes a **$1** withdrawal fee from the amount.

**Agents that trade on Polymarket.** Withdraw moves USDC off the Polymarket wallet and back to the agent's own wallet (`POST /polymarket/withdraw`). The amount is a decimal string with at most 6 decimal places — `1`, `1.00`, or `12.345678`. Extra zeros that don't change the value are fine (`1.2300000` is 1.23). An amount that isn't a positive number of millionths of a dollar (blank, not a number, zero, negative, or more precision than USDC has) is refused immediately with **422**, and nothing is queued. If a withdrawal for that agent is already in progress, the new request is refused with **409** until the first one finishes.

### Dust and unverified tokens

To keep your wallet easy to read, Fere hides two kinds of tokens by default. They're still in your wallet. Nothing is moved or deleted.

- **Dust**: any token worth less than **$2.00**. Small leftovers from trades and rounding build up over time, so we hide them. A holding quoted under **$0.10** is never listed, even when Hide dust is off.
- **Unverified**: a token flagged as a likely scam, or one with less than **$10,000** of trading liquidity. Anyone can send any token to any wallet, so unverified tokens are often "airdrops" you never asked for. Their price is also unreliable: a thin or fake market can show a value you could never actually sell for.

**To see them**, turn off **Hide dust** at the top of the holdings list in your wallet or in **Portfolio**. Unverified tokens then appear with an **Unverified** tag next to their quoted value. Anything quoted under **$0.10** stays hidden.

**What this means for your balance:**

- Unverified tokens are **never counted** in your totals or toward an agent's minimum, even when they're visible. The value on their row is a quote, not money you can count on.
- While **Hide dust** is on, your total only includes what you see, so dust of **$0.10** or more is left out. Turn it off to include those. Anything quoted under **$0.10** stays off the list.
- If an unverified token is worth more than **$2.00**, you can try to **Sell** it from the agent's page. Because these tokens have little liquidity, the trade may not always go through, and when it does you may get less than the quoted value. Some scam tokens can't be sold at all.

<Warning>
  If a token you don't recognize shows up in your wallet, don't interact with it, and never visit a website named in a token's name or description. These are common scam tactics.
</Warning>

## MCP Wallets

If you trade through the [Fere MCP](/mcp) from your own AI client, each account you create gets **two wallets**: one **EVM** wallet and one **Solana** wallet. To see their addresses or balances, just ask your AI — for example, *"What are my Fere wallet addresses?"*

## What Makes This Secure

- **Keys never exposed** — Private keys are generated and stored inside hardware-isolated enclaves.
- **Agents can only trade** — An agent can trade with its wallet, but only you can withdraw from it.
- **Policy enforcement** — Transaction rules are enforced at the enclave level.
- **Fast signing** — Wallet creation takes under 500ms. Transaction signing under 200ms.

**Infrastructure:** Coinbase CDP · Trusted Execution (TEE) · AWS Nitro Enclaves
